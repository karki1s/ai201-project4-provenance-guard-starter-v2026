# Acceptance criteria — Provenance Guard

Five criteria that say what "working" means for this service, written in unit 7
**before** you built it.

**All five are yours.** None are given.

An acceptance criterion names a number or something plainly observable. *"The
detector is accurate"* is an opinion — and perfect AI detection isn't possible
anyway, so it's an opinion about something nobody can deliver. *"Of 10 pieces I
wrote myself, at most 1 is labelled high-confidence AI"* is a criterion.

Under each, write a sentence or two on **why that target**. *"I allow at most 1
false positive in 10 because on a writing site a wrong accusation costs more
than a missed detection"* is a real answer.

> Missing your own targets next unit costs you nothing. Setting a target so
> easy you can't miss it does.

---

## Pick numbers you can defend

**Cover at least three of these five areas.** They're prompts, not a form.

| Area | A question it could answer |
|---|---|
| False positives | How often may a human's writing get called AI? |
| Score spread | How far apart should clearly-AI and clearly-human text score? |
| Label coverage | Is every one of your three labels actually reachable? |
| The appeal path | Does an appeal always change status and always get recorded? |
| Bad input | What happens when `text` is missing, empty, or enormous? |

Two things worth knowing before you pick numbers:

- **The expensive mistake is one-sided.** Missing some AI text costs the site a
  little. Accusing a real writer costs that person a lot. A criterion that
  treats both errors as equally bad is describing a system nobody would ship.
- **Unit 8 tests across three trials, and the target has to hold across all
  three.** Your first signal runs a model, so identical input can score
  differently twice. A target of "at most 1 in 10" against trials of 2, 1, 2 is
  a **miss**.

---
## 1. False positives on my own writing

Of 10 pieces I wrote myself without AI help, each 100–300 words and listed in at most 1 comes back from `/submit` with guess as AI. In every one of the three trials. At least 3 of the 10 are plain an average sentence length of 15 words or fewer.

**Why this target:** On a writing site a wrong accusation costs the writer far more than a missed detection costs the site. The plain pieces are there on purpose the model signal is known to score plain writing as machine like, so a test that only uses my own pieces would pass for the wrong reason.

---

## 2. Clearly AI and clearly human text score far apart

Two fixed sets of 5 texts each, 100–300 words, 
- **AI set:** generated by a chatbot from a one-line prompt, pasted unedited. The model and prompt are recorded next to each text.
- **Human set:** my own unedited writing, not reused from criterion 1.

The mean `confidence` returned by `/submit` for the AI set is at least 0.30 higher than the mean for the human set, in every trial.

**Why this target:** If the two groups sit close together, the labels are decided by noise, not by the text. A 0.30 gap is about the width of my unsure band (0.35–0.65), so anything smaller means even the easy cases could land in the same band.

---

## 3. Every label is reachable

A fixed set of 15 inputs, 5 of my own unedited writing, 5 unedited chatbot output, and 5 chatbot output I rewrote by hand
(about half the sentences changed). Each of the three `guess` values `"human"`, `"unsure"`, `"ai"` is returned for at least 2 of the 15, in every trial.

**Why this target:** A label nothing can reach is a gap in the thresholds, and a service that never says "unsure" is claiming certainty it doesn't have. Two, not one, so a single lucky score can't satisfy it.

---

## 4. Appeals always change status and always get recorded

Submit 5 texts, then appeal each `content_id` once with a non-empty `reasoning`. All 5 appeals return HTTP 200 with `"status": "under_review"`, and `GET /log` contains, for each, an entry with that `content_id` and
`"event": "appeal"`. Then 3 appeals on made up `content_id`s (valid UUID format, never submitted) all return HTTP 404, and `/log` gains no `"event": "appeal"`entry for them.

**Why this target:** The appeal is the only thing between a wrong accusation and nothing at all, so anything below 100% means some writer's appeal silently vanished. The fake-ID cases check it can't be recorded against nothing.

---

## 5. Bad input is refused cleanly, never crashes

6 malformed `/submit` requests, each with a valid `creator_id`: no `text` field, `"text": ""`, `"text": "   "`, `"text": null`,and  `"text": 42`. All 5 return a status between 400 and 499 (never 500) with a JSON body containing an `"error"` field, and each adds one entry with `"event": "rejected"` to `GET /log` — in every trial.

**Why this target:** A crash on malformed input is one of the most common real failures, and a 500 tells the caller nothing about what to fix. Logging the rejections matters because a refused request that leaves no trace looks the same as no request at all.
